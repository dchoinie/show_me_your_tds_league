# Show Me Your TDs — League Constitution

## 1. League Format

Show Me Your TDs is a dynasty fantasy football league. Unlike redraft leagues, where
rosters reset every season, dynasty teams retain their players year over year. Owners
build long-term franchises and are responsible for managing both their current
competitive window and the future health of their roster. New player acquisition each
year happens primarily through a rookie draft, free agency (FAAB), and trades — not a
full re-draft. Roster continuity is the core of the league.

## 2. Roster & Starting Lineup

Each team starts 9 players each week from a roster of 29 total players.

**Starting lineup (9):**

- 1 QB
- 2 RB
- 2 WR
- 1 TE
- 2 FLEX (RB/WR/TE)
- 1 SUPERFLEX (QB/RB/WR/TE)

**Reserves:**

- 14 bench spots
- 6 taxi squad spots

Total roster size: 29 players (9 starters + 14 bench + 6 taxi).

**Injured Reserve:** Up to 5 IR spots (see Section 7). IR spots are separate from the
29-player roster and do not count toward roster size; they hold only players who
officially carry NFL IR status.

## 3. Scoring

- Full PPR (1.0 point per reception)
- TE Premium: TEs receive an additional 0.5 PPR, for 1.5 points per reception
- All other scoring follows standard league defaults

## 4. Drafts

### Startup Draft

The inaugural draft is an auction-style draft. Every owner has the same auction budget
and may bid on any available player. The startup auction fills all 29 drafted roster
spots — the 23-player active roster (9 starters + 14 bench) plus the 6 taxi squad spots.

Because 29 drafted players exceed the 23-player active-roster limit, each owner must set
a valid roster before regular-season kickoff by designating taxi-eligible players (3 or
fewer years of NFL experience) to the taxi squad and/or dropping players, until the
active roster is within its 23-player limit. Any drafted player not moved to the taxi
squad or dropped counts against the active roster. The 5 IR spots are not drafted; they
are filled after the season begins via IR designations, waivers, and free agency.

### Rookie Drafts (annual, going forward)

After the startup, the only annual draft is a 4-round linear rookie draft, held in the
offseason. The draft is linear (non-snaking) — the same draft order is used in every
round (e.g., the team with the 1.01 also holds the 2.01, 3.01, and 4.01, barring trades).

### Postseason

The top 6 regular-season finishers enter the playoff bracket competing for the league
championship. Teams that miss the playoffs do not compete in a consolation bracket;
their rookie draft position is set by regular-season standings (see Draft Order below).

Playoff seeding is by regular-season record among the top 6 finishers, best record
earning the #1 seed.

**Bracket structure:** The top 2 seeds receive a first-round bye. The remaining four
seeds play in the first round (3 vs. 6, 4 vs. 5), and survivors advance to the
semifinals against the bye teams (1 vs. lowest remaining survivor, 2 vs. other).
Semifinal winners meet in the championship game. The two first-round losers play a
fifth-place game, and the two semifinal losers play a third-place game, so the bracket
produces a complete 1st-through-6th ordering.

### Draft Order

Rookie draft order is set entirely by finishing position. There is no lottery.

**Picks 1.01–1.06** go to the six teams that miss the playoffs, ordered by inverse
regular-season standings:

| Pick | Regular-season finish |
| --- | --- |
| 1.01 | 12th |
| 1.02 | 11th |
| 1.03 | 10th |
| 1.04 | 9th |
| 1.05 | 8th |
| 1.06 | 7th |

**Picks 1.07–1.12** go to the six playoff teams, ordered by inverse playoff finish:

| Pick | Playoff finish |
| --- | --- |
| 1.07 | 6th place |
| 1.08 | 5th place |
| 1.09 | 4th place |
| 1.10 | 3rd place |
| 1.11 | Runner-up |
| 1.12 | League champion |

Regular-season standings are ordered by record, with total points scored as the
tiebreaker.

### Draft Pick Trading

- Future rookie draft picks may be traded at any time the trade window is open.
- Picks may be traded up to three years out from the current season.

## 5. Setting Rosters

Owners are responsible for setting a valid starting lineup each week before kickoff of
each player's game.

A valid lineup means:

- A player is set in every starting position (no empty slots),
- Each starting player is projected for more than 0.1 points at the time of kickoff, AND
- No starter may be on a bye week, on IR, or carry an Out designation at the time of
  kickoff.

### Penalties for Invalid Lineups

| Offense | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| 1st | Warning |
| 2nd | Warning |
| 3rd and each subsequent offense | Forfeiture of the team's latest-round rookie pick in the next rookie draft |

Offenses accumulate across the season. Each additional offense beyond the third costs
the next available latest-round pick (e.g., 4th offense costs the next-latest pick still
owned).

## 6. Taxi Squad

The taxi squad is a 6-player developmental roster for young players.

- A player is taxi-eligible only if they have 3 or fewer years of NFL experience.
- Players may be moved onto the taxi squad only before the start of the regular season.
  Once Week 1 kicks off, no additions to the taxi squad are allowed for the remainder of
  the season.
- Taxi players may be promoted to the active roster at any time, but once promoted, they
  cannot return to the taxi squad that season.
- Taxi players do not count against the active roster and cannot be started.

## 7. Injured Reserve (IR)

- 5 IR spots.
- A player may only be placed on IR if their official NFL status is IR. Players
  designated as Out, Doubtful, Questionable, PUP, NFI, or Suspended do not qualify.
- When a player no longer holds IR status, the owner must move them off IR before the
  next kickoff or the slot is considered invalid.

## 8. Auto-Subs

- Each team has 2 auto-sub slots configured weekly.
- An auto-sub player must not play before the starter they would be replacing. If the
  auto-sub's game kicks off before the starter's, the substitution will not trigger and
  the starter remains locked in.

## 9. Free Agency & Waivers

- Waivers run on a FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget) system.
- Each team receives $100 FAAB at the start of the season.
- No minimum bid — bids of $0 are allowed.
- Tiebreakers on equal bids go to the team with the worse current standings / lower
  waiver priority (standard rolling priority).

## 10. Trades

- Trades are allowed between any two teams at any time during the open trade window.
- Players, FAAB, and draft picks (up to 3 years out) may all be traded.
- No trade vetoes will be issued except in cases of egregious collusion, as determined by
  the league commissioners. Lopsided trades that reflect different team-building
  philosophies or competitive windows are not subject to veto.
- Commissioners may investigate and reverse a trade only when there is clear evidence of
  collusion or an attempt to manipulate league competitiveness.

## 11. Commissioners

- Commissioners are responsible for enforcing this constitution, processing penalties,
  ruling on collusion, and resolving disputes not explicitly covered.
- Constitutional amendments require a majority vote of league owners. The commissioners
  may make minor clarifying edits without a vote, provided they do not change the
  substance of any rule.
